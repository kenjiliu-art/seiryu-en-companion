# Seiryū-en Companion

*A digital companion to the James Irvine Japanese Garden*

Version 1.0
Draft

---

# Purpose

Seiryū-en Companion is a digital companion to the James Irvine Japanese Garden at the Japanese American Cultural & Community Center (JACCC).

Its purpose is not to replace the experience of visiting the garden.

Its purpose is to help visitors notice more deeply, understand more fully, and return more often.

The garden is always the primary experience.

The app simply accompanies the visitor.

Technology is not the project. Stewardship is the project. Technology is simply one of the tools that allows that stewardship to continue.

---

# Our Mission

Help people experience Japanese gardens through observation, story, seasonality, and cultural context.

Every feature should encourage visitors to look away from the phone and back toward the garden.

Authenticity is more important than novelty. The purpose of Seiryū-en Companion is to reveal the documented richness of the garden, not to invent new meanings for it.

---

# Guiding Question

Every new feature should answer one question:

> Does this help someone experience the real garden more deeply?

If the answer is no, it probably does not belong in this project.

---

# Design Principles

## The garden comes first.

The phone should never become the center of attention.

The app should continually encourage visitors to:

- look up
- walk
- listen
- observe
- return

---

## Calm over complexity.

The interface should feel spacious and quiet.

Avoid:

- unnecessary animation
- excessive color
- visual clutter
- information overload

Every screen should leave room to breathe.

---

## Observation before explanation.

Whenever possible, encourage visitors to notice something before explaining it.

Example:

Instead of:

"This tree is..."

Prefer:

"Notice how the branches reach toward the light."

Then explain.

---

## Small pieces.

Information should be presented in small, readable sections.

Avoid long walls of text.

Prefer:

- one paragraph
- one photograph
- one poem
- one observation
- one story

---

## Every visit should feel different.

The garden changes.

The companion should change with it.

Future features may include:

- seasonal observations
- rotating poems
- today's invitation
- changing photography
- current blooms

---

# Interpretation Principles

## Evidence before interpretation

Seiryū-en Companion is an interpretive resource grounded in documented history.

The project should distinguish between:

### Documented Design Intent

Information that is supported by original design documents, archival records, published scholarship, interviews with the garden's designer, or other primary sources.

Examples include:

- the original design concept
- the symbolism intentionally incorporated into the garden
- historical construction information
- documented planting decisions

---

### Institutional Knowledge

Knowledge shared by JACCC, the Southern California Gardeners Federation, identified gardeners, curators, and other recognized contributors.

Whenever practical, these contributions should be attributed.

---

### Visitor Observation

The companion may encourage visitors to notice aspects of the garden that are directly observable.

Examples include:

- changes in light
- seasonal color
- the sound of water
- relationships between nearby features

Observation prompts should encourage attention without introducing undocumented symbolic meanings.

---

### Interpretation Policy

The project should not invent symbolism, historical narratives, or cultural meanings that are not supported by evidence.

Where uncertainty exists, it should be acknowledged.

Where multiple documented interpretations exist, they may be presented together with appropriate context.

The long-term goal is to create a trustworthy resource that reflects the garden's documented history while remaining welcoming and accessible to the public.

# Editing Philosophy

Content editors should never need to write code.

The editing experience should be:

- visual
- simple
- forgiving

Staff should be able to:

- edit text
- upload images
- move map markers
- add stories
- publish changes

without understanding the underlying technology.

---

# Technology Philosophy

This project should remain as independent as possible from any single platform.

The website may change.

The CMS may change.

WordPress may eventually be replaced.

The content should remain portable.

Whenever practical:

- content should be stored separately from presentation
- data formats should remain simple
- migration should be straightforward

---

# GitHub (Plain Language)

GitHub stores the project's history.

Think of it as a complete record of every change ever made.

It allows developers to:

- save work safely
- undo mistakes
- collaborate
- recover older versions

GitHub is not the website.

Visitors never use GitHub.

It exists only to help maintain the project.

If staff members change in the future, the GitHub account should be transferred to the next person responsible for maintaining the project.

# Success

The project succeeds when visitors leave the garden having noticed something they would otherwise have missed.

The app is successful when it quietly disappears and the visitor's attention returns to the garden.

# Curatorial Philosophy

Seiryū-en Companion is not a plant identification app, a general reference work, or a replacement for the experience of visiting the garden.

It is an interpretive companion created in support of JACCC's mission to preserve, present, and share Japanese and Japanese American cultural heritage.

Every feature should strengthen the visitor's relationship with the physical garden.

The project values:

- historical accuracy
- cultural integrity
- careful scholarship
- accessibility
- thoughtful observation
- respect for original sources

over novelty, entertainment, or unnecessary technological complexity.

The companion should remain quiet enough that visitors spend more time looking at the garden than looking at their phones.

---

## The Garden Is the Primary Object

The physical garden is the exhibition.

The companion exists only to deepen that experience.

Visitors should be encouraged to:

- observe before reading
- return to the landscape frequently
- experience the garden with all of their senses
- discover relationships between plants, water, stone, architecture, history, and community

The companion should continually return attention to the garden itself.

---

## Curatorial Responsibility

Seiryū-en Companion represents JACCC's interpretation of the garden.

Because of that responsibility, new content should meet the same standards expected of exhibitions, publications, and collections stewardship.

Whenever practical:

- factual statements should be supported by documented sources
- quotations should be attributed
- photographs should include provenance
- contributors should be acknowledged
- revisions should preserve an editorial history

The companion should be considered a living publication rather than a collection of web pages.

---

# Relationship to the Living Garden

Seiryū-en is not a historic site frozen in time.

It is a living garden that continues to grow, mature, and be cared for.

The companion should document both:

- the original design intent
- the continuing stewardship that allows the garden to thrive today

Gardening decisions, seasonal changes, replacement plantings, conservation work, and community care are part of the garden's ongoing history and should be documented when appropriate.

---

# Long-Term Vision

The long-term goal of Seiryū-en Companion is to become the authoritative digital companion to the James Irvine Japanese Garden.

It should support casual visitors, students, scholars, gardeners, and future generations while remaining faithful to the documented history and design of the garden.

The project should be able to evolve as new research, new documentation, and new community knowledge become available, without losing the clarity, accuracy, and humility that define its purpose.
